
# F027: Anonymous chat with PIN protection

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Branch:** Not created
- **Pull request:** Not created

## Goal

Let signed-in users invite another account to a private chat while displaying chosen aliases instead of account identities in the conversation. Require a separate six-digit PIN to open anonymous chats.

## User flow

1. A signed-in user enters another user's email to request a chat.
2. The recipient accepts or declines. Participants choose display aliases and set a chat PIN.
3. A participant enters their PIN to open chats. Four wrong attempts trigger a 25-minute lockout.
4. A participant may use **Delete all my anonymous chats** after confirming its effect.

## In scope

- Explicit acceptance before a conversation becomes active.
- Alias-based message display; the server retains account identity for access checks and abuse controls.
- PIN creation, verification, reset path, failed-attempt counting, and lockout.
- Participant-only message reads and sends; neutral invitation response that does not reveal account existence.
- Lockout, invalid PIN, declined request, and empty-chat states.
- A destructive control whose exact deletion scope needs the decision below.

## Out of scope

- Anonymity from the service operator, infrastructure logs, or a recipient who already knows the invitation email.
- Public account search, contact lists, Family chat (F024), attachments, and end-to-end encryption.

## Wireframe or UI changes

Add **Anonymous Chat** with invite-by-email, incoming requests, chats, PIN gate, alias settings, and message thread. The deletion control must state exactly what it removes and require confirmation; do not promise “no traces” until retention is decided.

## API changes (proposal)

- `POST /api/chat/requests`: invite by email with a neutral response for unknown accounts.
- `GET /api/chat/requests` and `POST /api/chat/requests/{id}/accept` or `/decline`: recipient request management.
- `POST /api/chat/pin` and `POST /api/chat/unlock`: set and verify PIN with server-side attempt limits.
- `GET /api/chat/conversations` and `GET` / `POST /api/chat/conversations/{id}/messages`: participant-only access after PIN unlock.
- `DELETE /api/chat/conversations`: follow the approved deletion policy.
- Errors must not expose user existence, PIN details, or other participants' chats.

## MongoDB changes (proposal)

Store invitation requests, participant User IDs, aliases, conversations, messages, a hashed PIN verifier, failed-attempt count, and lockout expiry. Use the existing users collection for email lookup. Never store a raw PIN. Retention and audit metadata need approval.

## Architecture decisions

- “Anonymous” describes the identity shown to the other participant, not anonymous access to the service.
- Require normal authentication and server-side rate limits in addition to the PIN.
- Keep F027 separate from F024; Family membership does not grant chat access.

## Acceptance criteria

- The recipient must accept before messages can be exchanged.
- Conversation displays aliases without account names or email addresses.
- A third account cannot enumerate or read requests, conversations, or messages.
- Four incorrect PIN entries block unlock for 25 minutes, including after a reload.
- Deleted chats become inaccessible under the approved deletion policy.

## Local verification

Test invitation acceptance, unknown-email response, participant isolation, PIN attempt counting and expiry, and deletion with two browser sessions and an unrelated account.

## Open questions

1. Does the recipient see the sender's account identity before accepting, or only an alias?
2. Is the PIN per account or per conversation? How is a forgotten PIN reset?
3. Is lockout always 25 minutes, or do repeated lockouts increase it? The earlier draft specified both a fixed cooldown and exponential backoff.
4. Does **Delete all** remove chats for both participants or only the caller? What audit records must remain, and for how long?
5. May users block invitations from another account or opt out entirely?
